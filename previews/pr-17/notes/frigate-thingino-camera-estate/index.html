Building a low-cost Frigate camera estate with Thingino

Project period · July 2026

Scaling a mixed Thingino/Tapo/Reolink camera estate to fourteen units under one Frigate instance stayed affordable and low-maintenance once onboarding, config backup and PTZ control were made repeatable.

  • Frigate
  • Cameras
  • Home Lab
  • Home Assistant

In brief

  • A camera estate grew organically from a handful of cheap Thingino-firmware cameras to fourteen mixed cameras under one Frigate instance, without a big-vendor subscription.
  • A near-identical config pattern for each Thingino camera (go2rtc streams, ONVIF host, detect/record roles) made adding new units mostly mechanical, backed up and restored through Frigate’s own config API.
  • A brief CPU-saturation incident, caused by several cameras being silently force-transcoded, was fixed the same way the direct-RTSP note describes; see that note for the diagnosis and fix, not repeated here.
  • Frigate’s own pan/tilt arrow controls worked for both PTZ-capable Thingino units and a motorized Tapo TC70, making a separate Home Assistant ONVIF integration optional rather than required.
  • The same repeatable onboarding pattern that scaled the estate to fourteen cameras is directly reusable for any future camera addition.

Question

Consumer NVR subscriptions and proprietary camera ecosystems are expensive and lock you into a vendor’s app. The alternative attempted here was to combine Frigate — a self-hosted, Coral-accelerated open-source NVR — with a growing mix of genuinely cheap cameras: Cinnado/Thingino D1-chipset units running open replacement firmware, plus a handful of consumer Tapo and Reolink cameras already in use around the house. The practical question, once the estate had already grown past a handful of cameras, was whether a mixed, incrementally-grown estate could keep scaling reliably — onboarding new units, surviving config changes, and supporting PTZ hardware from different vendors — without either a big recurring subscription or constant manual firefighting.

Setup

Frigate ran on the home Unraid server, backed by a Coral USB accelerator for object detection, with the camera estate accessed through Frigate’s own live config-save-and-restart API (POST /api/config/save?save_option=restart). Cameras fell into three families: Cinnado/Thingino PTZ units (identified over the LAN by their thingino HTTP title and RTSP port 554), fixed and motorized Tapo units (TC60/TC70/C520WS, identified via ONVIF port 2020), and a Reolink PoE doorbell. Each camera got a go2rtc main/sub stream pair, detection running from the lighter substream and recording from the full main stream, plus camera-group membership for the dashboard. By this stage the estate had grown to fourteen cameras across these three families.

What we tried

A repeatable per-camera onboarding pattern. Adding a new Thingino camera became close to mechanical: light-scan the expected IP range for the device’s mDNS name and open ports, back up the live Frigate config (both raw and decoded YAML) before any change, generate a candidate config mirroring the existing Thingino pattern (go2rtc raw and ffmpeg-wrapped streams, ONVIF host, detect/record roles, camera-group membership), apply it through the API, then verify: config lists the new camera, the latest-snapshot endpoint returns a real JPEG, and camera/detection FPS settle at the expected rate. This pattern, repeated for each new camera as they were brought online through the estate’s growth, kept onboarding low-risk because every change had a config diff, a backup, and a positive functional check rather than “it looks fine in the preview.”

Discovering PTZ control was already solved. A new Tapo TC70 with motorized pan/tilt initially suggested a need for Home Assistant’s native ONVIF integration to get dashboard PTZ buttons. It turned out Frigate’s own live-view arrow controls already drove pan/tilt on both the new TC70 and the existing Thingino PTZ units, without any extra integration. HA ONVIF stayed an optional dashboard-convenience layer rather than the assumed primary control path — a case where the simpler existing tool already did the job the more elaborate plan assumed was necessary.

A brief CPU incident, resolved by an already-documented fix. As the estate grew past a dozen cameras, several Thingino main/sub stream aliases ended up silently force-transcoded through software libx264 instead of direct RTSP, sending host CPU to roughly 98-100%. The diagnosis and fix — inspecting go2rtc’s stream configuration, switching the affected aliases back to direct RTSP, and verifying the CPU and temperature recovery — are covered in full in Direct RTSP versus transcoding; the fix here was the same pattern applied to newly onboarded cameras that had inherited the same forced-ffmpeg alias by copy-paste from an older config.

Interoperability across three PTZ implementations. Once the estate included Thingino PTZ units, a Tapo TC70, and ONVIF-capable hardware side by side, Frigate’s own live-view arrow controls turned out to drive pan/tilt identically across all three, without per-vendor glue code. This mattered for scaling: adding a differently-branded PTZ camera didn’t need a new integration path, just the same onboarding pattern applied once more.

Result

By the point covered here, fourteen cameras spanning Thingino, Tapo and Reolink hardware ran under one Frigate instance at roughly 5 fps camera/process/detection rate each, with Coral inference typically single-digit milliseconds. Retention was tuned deliberately (continuous 1→2 days, motion 3→6 days) after confirming ample headroom on the media share, rather than left at defaults or increased blindly. Scaling three times over — from a handful of cameras to fourteen, across three camera vendors and two PTZ implementations — never required abandoning the original repeatable onboarding pattern.

What we would do next

The onboarding pattern proven here — light network scan, config backup, candidate diff, API apply, functional verification — is reusable for any future camera addition without needing to reinvent the process, regardless of vendor or PTZ capability.
