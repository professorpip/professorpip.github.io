Layered physical recovery with a servo button-presser and JetKVM

Project period · July-August 2026

When wake-on-LAN couldn't reliably bring a PC back after shutdown, two independent hardware layers, a servo pressing the power button and a JetKVM giving full remote screen and keyboard access, closed the gap.

  • Hardware Reuse
  • Reliability
  • Home Lab
  • Automation

In brief

  • Wake-on-LAN worked from a Windows shutdown but stayed unreliable from an Ubuntu shutdown on the same PC, even after several genuine fixes.
  • A small Arduino-plus-servo rig, clamped over a laptop’s power button, powers the machine on regardless of OS or network state.
  • A separate JetKVM device gives full remote screen, keyboard, mouse and BIOS access, so recovery can continue past power-on into fixing a stuck boot.
  • Getting the servo reliable took real hardware debugging: capacitor filtering, a serial double-open bug, and manual angle tuning.
  • The two layers stay independent by design. JetKVM’s own power controls are unapproved for automation, so the servo remains a working fallback.

Question

Wake-on-LAN, remote power management and cloud out-of-band tools all assume something on the machine or network is still cooperating. What happens when a home-lab PC is simply off, or stuck in a state where none of the software wake paths work?

Ubuntu-initiated shutdown on a Ryzen PC left wake-on-LAN unreliable. Fixes to standby power, Netplan WoL settings, PCIe wake, Energy-Efficient Ethernet, and the BIOS “Restore AC Power Loss” setting all failed to resolve it. If none of that guaranteed a wake, the fix had to stop depending on the OS at all: a physical path to pressing the power button.

Setup

The build target was a laptop: an ATmega168-based Arduino board over FTDI USB serial, driving a small SG90 micro-servo through an expansion shield. A separate JetKVM IP-KVM unit was already deployed on the LAN for another PC needing full remote screen, keyboard, mouse and BIOS access.

What we tried

A simple physical actuator. A servo horn clamps over the laptop’s power button and presses it on command. A sketch listens for single-character commands: P to press, R/U to rest, D to hold down, T to test a double-press.

Hardware debugging. The servo initially jittered and caused what looked like resets. A 1000µF capacitor across the 5V/GND rails fixed it, confirmed by a clean sweep test. A first press command reported success but produced no movement. Watching the raw serial exchange showed the Arduino printed READY, received P, and replied PRESS. The command path worked, but the shell script opened the serial port twice, causing a reset right as the command sent. The fix held one file descriptor open throughout, then kept the port open a few seconds longer after each command to stop it closing mid-motion.

Mechanical tuning. The press direction was initially backwards, moving away from the button rather than into it. Swapping which angle counted as rest versus press, then adjusting the press angle and the servo horn’s mounting position, got a reliable press — confirmed by watching the target machine actually boot.

A second target. The same servo concept became the candidate physical-recovery option for the Ryzen PC’s Ubuntu-side wake problem too. JetKVM’s own power controls and a deeper BIOS/OS wake audit are parked for later.

Layered, not merged, safety boundaries. JetKVM exposes keyboard, mouse, virtual-media, power, USB-emulation, reboot and wake-on-LAN tools through its own automation interface. Power, reboot and WoL actions stayed unapproved for automated use; only screenshot/read-only access is approved by default. That keeps the servo as a genuinely independent recovery layer. Physical power-on doesn’t depend on JetKVM’s automation, and once a machine is powered on, JetKVM still provides full remote screen/keyboard/mouse access, including BIOS setup.

Result

The servo reliably powers on its laptop target, confirmed by watching it boot after a single press command. The full serial path — one held-open connection, correct hold timing, corrected angle and mounting — works end to end. JetKVM was separately proven for BIOS-level access on the Ryzen PC, used to inspect and adjust real BIOS settings, including AC-power-restore, without a keyboard or monitor attached. Together the two tools answer “the PC won’t come back and nothing software-based is helping”: press the button physically first, then take full remote control once it’s on.

What we would do next

The most direct follow-up is adapting the servo mechanism to the Ryzen PC, since its Ubuntu-shutdown WoL problem was the concrete trigger for treating physical recovery as a real requirement. The other is deciding, deliberately, whether JetKVM’s own power/reboot/WoL automation should ever be approved for unattended use, given the servo already provides an independent, lower-privilege path to the same outcome.
