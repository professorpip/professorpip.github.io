The health check that briefly broke Zigbee

Project period · July 2026

Two eight-second outages showed why connecting to a live serial bridge is not a harmless port check.

  • Home Assistant
  • Zigbee
  • Reliability
  • Monitoring

In brief

  • A routine ZHA check opened the coordinator’s TCP serial port to prove it was reachable.
  • Every monitored Zigbee entity then became unavailable for roughly eight seconds.
  • The same pattern happened twice and correlated with the port probe.
  • Safe monitoring now reads Home Assistant state and inspects the remote USB/process over SSH without connecting to the serial listener.
  • The watchdog retries after 15 seconds before alerting on a transient outage.

Home Assistant’s Zigbee Home Automation (ZHA) integration controlled the mesh through a Sonoff Zigbee 3.0 USB Dongle Plus. The dongle was attached to an OpenWRT access point and exposed to Home Assistant by ser2net as a live, effectively single-client serial connection. The health check used a familiar test: open its TCP port and see whether it answers. In this architecture that connection was not passive observation; it briefly competed with ZHA for the radio.

The suspicious pattern

During a morning check, all monitored ZHA devices suddenly appeared unavailable. The remote access point was healthy, ser2net was running and the USB device existed. About eight seconds later, entity values were fresh again.

The afternoon check produced the same sequence: healthy infrastructure, a complete but brief ZHA disappearance, then recovery. A random mesh problem rarely aligns that neatly with two diagnostic sessions.

Both sessions had included a generic TCP port probe against the active ser2net listener. The listener represented a live bidirectional serial connection to the coordinator, likely with one useful client. Opening it could evict or disturb Home Assistant’s connection. ZHA then marked the network unavailable until it reconnected.

Change the observer

Routine checks stopped touching the serial port. They now:

  • read selected entity states and timestamps from Home Assistant;
  • inspect the remote ser2net process and USB device over SSH;
  • review remote logs for current USB or serial errors;
  • avoid telnet, netcat and ad-hoc socket opens.

The daily watchdog also gained a 15-second second pass. It still reports persistent unavailable, unknown or stale states; it suppresses a short recoverable blip. A live run then returned cleanly without connecting to the coordinator socket.

Result

The Zigbee network had not developed a mysterious twice-daily fault. Its monitor had become an active participant.

The lesson extends beyond Zigbee: a TCP endpoint can front a database session, industrial controller, console or single-client serial line. “Port open” may consume, reset or steal the resource. Prefer application-level state and out-of-band process checks; probe the live endpoint only when reconnect behaviour is the experiment.
