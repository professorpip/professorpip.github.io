The 16 GB VRAM local-model shootout

Project period · June–September 2026

What months of local-model tests taught us about speed, quality, fit and dependable agent work on one consumer GPU.

  • Local AI
  • Benchmarking
  • Inference
  • Ryzen

In brief

  • We tested models from 12B to large MoE designs on an RTX 5060 Ti with 16 GB VRAM.
  • Short-prompt speed was a poor predictor of dependable agent work.
  • Qwen Coder exceeded 120 tokens/s in the first bench, yet later evaluation showed that specialisation did not transfer cleanly.
  • Flash Next won academic and judgement tests; a 27B Qwen variant remained the more reliable default agent.
  • The useful result was a portfolio of profiles, not one universal winner.

A 16 GB graphics card can run surprisingly large quantised models. That does not mean every model which loads is useful.

The first lap

Our June bench used the same Windows machine, CUDA build and OpenAI-compatible llama.cpp endpoint. Gemma 4 12B used about 8.6 GB after loading and generated roughly 52–56 tokens/s. Qwen3-Coder 30B A3B occupied about 14.6 GB but exceeded 120 tokens/s on every short task. A 35B MoE Qwen sat between them. Gemma 4 26B loaded at roughly 15.9 GB and crawled at about 22–24 tokens/s.

All could follow an exact-token instruction and write a tiny function. Most mishandled a deliberately simple percentage calculation. “It fits” and “it answers” were only entry tickets.

Better gates changed the winner

We added a 17-task local-agent suite, strict file verifiers, long-context retrieval, a GCSE-derived benchmark and repeated harness runs. Results stopped lining up neatly.

The coding model was quick, but scored 265/361 on the text-only GCSE route. Gemma 12B scored 227/361 and exposed long-prompt failures. Flash Next IQ3 led that route at 340/361 and scored 17/17 on a compact judgement test, yet managed only 11/17 clean local-agent tasks with four critical errors. The Dirk-tuned Qwen3.8 27B profile scored lower academically—313/361—but delivered 14/17 clean agent tasks and 15/15 on the reliability subset.

That distinction mattered. The default worker needed to respect missing information, filenames, JSON contracts and tool arguments more than it needed to maximise an exam score.

The result

We kept roles instead of crowning a champion:

  • a Qwen3.8 27B profile for dependable everyday agent work;
  • Flash Next as a strong planner or reviewer;
  • a roomier long-context Qwen profile for large inputs;
  • small Mac-hosted Qwen as an emergency fallback;
  • Gemma as a known baseline rather than the default.

The shootout became less about a leaderboard and more about routing. Benchmark the behaviour you intend to deploy, preserve failures, and let different models earn different jobs.
